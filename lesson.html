Lesson 1: Introduction to ICT

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) refers to technologies used to create, collect, process, store, retrieve, and communicate information.

Information

Facts, knowledge, and data.

Communication

Texting, messaging, phone calls, and video calls.

Technology

Tools, devices, and machines.

Examples of ICT tools

  • Devices: laptop, smartphone, printer, smartwatch, router, USB flash drive
  • Services and apps: Google Drive, Facebook, GCash, Google Maps

These tools help us communicate, store data, access the internet, and solve everyday problems.

Components of ICT

The World Wide Web and the Internet

The internet links billions of computers, phones, and smart devices so they can communicate and share data instantly. The World Wide Web (WWW) is the collection of web pages we open through the internet using a browser.

Lesson 2: Different types of websites

Static website

Fixed content that is the same for all visitors. It only displays information and usually needs a web developer to update it.

Dynamic website

Content changes based on user interaction, time, or database information. It is interactive and updated regularly.

Lesson 3: ICT careers, digital citizenship, and online safety

ICT sectors and careers: the people behind ICT

Programmer

Creates and maintains computer programs and software using programming languages.

Web developer

Designs, builds, and maintains websites and web applications.

Graphic designer

Creates posters, logos, advertisements, and digital graphics.

Animator

Creates moving images and visual effects for films, games, and ads.

Cybersecurity specialist

Protects computers, networks, systems, and data from cyberattacks and security threats.

Data analyst

Helps organizations organize, manage, and understand data to make better decisions.

E-commerce is the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet through digital devices and platforms.

Digital citizenship and netiquette

Digital citizenship means using technology responsibly, respectfully, safely, and appropriately. Netiquette comes from etiquette + network: the rules of online courtesy.

  • Remember the human. A real person is behind the screen.
  • Follow real-life standards. Honesty, respect, responsibility, and appropriate behavior apply online too.
Example: Before posting a comment, ask: "Would I say this to this person's face?" If not, do not post it.

Cyberbullying and cyber threats

Cyberbullying

Using digital platforms to harass, shame, or threaten someone. What to do: do not reply, save screenshots, block the sender, and tell a trusted adult or teacher.

Phishing

Fake emails or websites that trick users into revealing passwords or card numbers.

Example: A fake bank email asking you to click a link and "verify" your account.

Spam

Unwanted bulk messages, often with harmful links or ads.

Malware

Malicious software such as viruses, worms, trojans, spyware, ransomware, and adware.

Online security and protection

  • Use strong, different passwords and turn on two-step verification.
  • Keep your operating system and antivirus updated.
  • Do not open unknown links or attachments.
  • Think before you share personal information.
  • Download apps only from trusted sources.
